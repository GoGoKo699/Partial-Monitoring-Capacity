# Preserved development record

The preliminary monitoring calculation used a valid but loose input-entropy cut. Its first run failed to bracket the interior crossing for exactly zero collected weight: there is no such crossing in that endpoint case. The endpoint is now handled exactly. The failed source, log and report remain; no tolerance or physical model changed.

The complete-rate derivation then replaced the loose cut with the stronger B+D quantum-output cut and used the standard assistance theorem to attain their minimum. This is a substantive strengthening, not a renamed numerical upper bound. The first enriched script had one extra closing parenthesis and did not execute. After that syntax repair, one near-threshold slope check failed: the new exact rate approached its limit more slowly than the loose upper bound whose test grid had been reused. The finite-epsilon data are retained and a smaller epsilon was appended. The tolerance and analytical formula were not changed.

The finite-spin suite initially attempted double-grid time optimization at 16 spins. Two executions exceeded their limits (45 and 120 seconds) during that group, produced no final report, and are not counted as completed validation. The declared completed study uses 8--12 spins. Its assertions and time interval are unchanged. The 16-spin script and logs are preserved to show the scope change.

Both completed spin runs pass every scientific assertion. They are not byte-identical: one maximum-conservation-error field differs by 3.64e-14, while all physical results and the remainder of the JSON are identical. The first regression wrapper stopped on its exact-byte assertion. The completion wrapper explicitly records the only field difference and runs the untouched prior suites. No report was overwritten or refreshed.

No failed calculation is counted as a pass. The final monitoring seven groups repeat exactly, the finite-spin four groups pass with the stated diagnostic difference, and the original six-plus-three groups reproduce their reports exactly. This remains author-side verification, not external scientific review.
