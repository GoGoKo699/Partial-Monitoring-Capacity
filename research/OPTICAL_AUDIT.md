## 1. The fixed communication task and the result audited

An isometry sends each input to B, the receiver; E, permanently inaccessible radiation; and D, the helper's collected radiation. The helper may measure arbitrarily many D outputs jointly and send the classical result X **only to B**. The encoder and decoder may operate on many uses. No quantum transmission of D, preshared entanglement, feedback, intermediate control of the decay, postselection, or two-way distillation is supplied. Coherent memory and processing at the helper, phase references, and the classical message length are unrestricted resources. This is an asymptotic entanglement-transmission task, not a finite-code or hardware-performance claim.

The previous finite-dimensional statement assumes the **channel identity**

\[
\mathcal N_{ED}=(\mathcal T_{B\to E}\otimes\mathrm{id}_D)\mathcal N_{BD}.
\tag{1}
\]

This equality preserves the joint D correlations, every input reference, and tensor powers. It is stronger than the marginal statement N_E=T N_B. Its role is that after any measurement of D, the *same* channel T still simulates the conditional E output from B.

Under (1), the supplied result is

\[
Q_{D\to B}^{\rm meas}=\max_{\rho_A}\{\min[S(B),S(BD)]-S(E)\}.
\tag{2}
\]

For the three-way qubit decay, a is survival, b uncollected decay and c collected decay, with a+b+c=1. The strong simulation is amplitude damping with survival b/a when a>=b. Equation (2) yields the existing qubit formula

\[
Q_{\rm qubit}=\max_{q\in[0,1]}\{\min[h_2(aq),h_2((1-b)q)]-h_2(bq)\}
\quad(a>b),
\]

and the reversed simulation proves zero capacity when a<=b. At a=.2,b=.08,c=.72, the existing value .3057095431400063 qubits/use and q=25/28 remain unchanged.

## 2. Resource audit: why achievability does not secretly act on E or send a result to the encoder

A rank-one refinement of the helper's measurement is sufficient: the receiver can ignore extra information. Purify a fixed input rho_A using a reference R. Each conditional RBE state after that measurement is pure, so the **actual measured channel**, including its classical output, has coherent information

\[
I_c=S(B^n|X)-S(E^n|X).
\]

The flagged complement can carry a copy of X as well; a classical record is not a secret quantum side channel. No unobserved event is discarded.

The achievability construction has three ordered steps.

1. Apply the pure-state entanglement-of-assistance ensemble theorem [SVW05, Theorem 1] to the mathematical grouping B | (RE) | D. It supplies rank-one block measurements on D^n satisfying

\[
\sum_xp_x S(B_x^n)\ge n\{\min[S(B),S(BD)]-\epsilon\}.
\]

This is a statement about the conditional ensemble generated by measuring D. The proof does **not** require that RE subsequently be manipulated as an accessible party.

2. Independently, entropy concavity gives

\[
\sum_xp_xS(E_x^n)\le nS(E).
\]

The actual flagged channel A^n -> B^nX therefore has coherent information at least the right side of (2), times n, minus n epsilon.

3. **Freeze that helper measurement** and treat n physical uses as one channel use. Ordinary quantum channel coding across many such blocks achieves its coherent information. Only the decoder receives X. The helper measurement can depend on the designed input density matrix and code specification, not on the unknown message or a later action by the sender.

This is the channel-coding construction of [SVW05, Theorem 8], applied after the partial-environment coherent-information subtraction. [DH11, Theorem 8] already identifies the related minimum-cut coherent-information assistance lower bound. Its broader recipient-LOCC task is not directly imported as a receiver-only protocol. The previous note's separate flagged-channel argument is needed, and survives this check.

For the converse, (1) implies data processing between the two classical-quantum marginals X B^n and X E^n:

\[
\begin{aligned}
I_c&=S(B^n)-S(E^n)-[I(X;B^n)-I(X;E^n)]\\
&\le S(B^n)-S(E^n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Coherent delivery of BD can only increase the receiver's resources, yielding the second cut S(B^nD^n)-S(E^n). The concavity/subadditivity arguments in the input note then give (2) for arbitrary collective encodings.

**Terminology clarification:** (1) supplies the required data-processing comparison. It need not make the *actual* tripartite state XBE a quantum Markov chain with vanishing conditional mutual information. No such stronger assertion is needed. This clarifies the shorthand in the preceding note without changing a capacity formula.

The two-use random-POVM tests verify the conditional degrading identity and both cuts for non-diagonal inputs. They are finite identity checks, not a numerical proof of assistance coding or an enumeration of all measurements.

## 3. Marginal degradability alone would give a false theorem

A standard random-unitary correction example [GW03] makes the distinction operational. Define

\[
W|\psi\rangle=
\frac{|\psi\rangle_B|0\rangle_E|0\rangle_D
+Z|\psi\rangle_B|1\rangle_E|1\rangle_D}{\sqrt2}.
\]

B alone receives a fully dephased qubit; E alone is I/2 independently of the input. Thus a replacement channel maps B's **marginal** to E. Incorrectly using only that marginal relation in (3) would predict a nonpositive coherent-information bound, because S(B)-S(E)<=0.

But measuring D in its displayed basis identifies whether Z was applied. Sending that bit to the receiver permits exact recovery, and the communication rate is one qubit/use.

There is no contradiction to (1). BD has no off-diagonal D blocks. At the input |0>, ED has an off-diagonal matrix element |00><11| of magnitude 1/2. No map acting solely on B can create that missing D coherence. The joint register-preserving relation fails.

This is not a newly discovered correction mechanism or a counterexample to the stated theorem. It shows that its hypothesis is substantive and must not be simplified to ordinary marginal degradability in presentation.

## 4. New result: an exact photon-budget law for monitored optical loss

Consider one bosonic signal mode with all ancillary *input* ports in vacuum. A passive network splits its excitation amplitude among B,E,D:

\[
\hat A^\dagger\longmapsto
\sqrt a\,\hat B^\dagger+\sqrt b\,\hat E^\dagger+\sqrt c\,\hat D^\dagger,
\qquad a+b+c=1.
\tag{4}
\]

Equivalently, on each number state,

\[
V|n\rangle=
\sum_{i+j+k=n}
\sqrt{\frac{n!}{i!j!k!}}\,a^{i/2}b^{j/2}c^{k/2}
|i,j,k\rangle_{BED}.
\]

The helper resources are exactly those in Section 1. In particular, preparing nonvacuum input states at an environmental port is not allowed, and a measurement need not be Gaussian. Let N be the permitted average incident signal photon number per use, averaged over the code's message state and channel uses. This is the ordinary average-energy constraint, not a maximum photon number in every codeword or a total budget including helper apparatus and local oscillators.

Write

\[
g(x)=(x+1)\log_2(x+1)-x\log_2x,\qquad g(0)=0.
\]

**Author-side theorem:**

\[
\boxed{
Q_{\rm optical}^{\rm meas}(N;a,b,c)=
\begin{cases}
g(aN)-g(bN),&a>b,\\
0,&a\le b.
\end{cases}}
\tag{5}
\]

The converse includes arbitrary non-Gaussian input states, entanglement across uses, nonuniform allocation of input energy, and arbitrary collective helper measurements. Achievability is asymptotic; it does not construct a finite low-complexity receiver. A thermal **average encoded input** is optimal. Sending an uncoded thermal beam is not a quantum code, and the environment remains vacuum, not a thermal-noise bath.

### 4.1 Joint degradation on the entire optical Fock space

For a>=b, further vacuum loss of transmissivity b/a acting on B, with D untouched, produces exactly ED. This follows by composing passive splitter transformations, or on the number-state operator basis and continuity. It is not only a relation between Gaussian covariances. The finite-excitation checks retain arbitrary input coherences and verify the identity after non-Gaussian rank-one helper measurements.

For a<=b the reverse map gives the prior antidegradability converse, so the capacity is zero, independently of incident energy. The all-zero a=b endpoint is direct and does not use a division by zero.

### 4.2 An all-input entropy bound without assuming Gaussian optimality

Let an n-use input have actual mean energy barN<=N per use. Its output energies are n a barN and n b barN. For x>0 let tau_x be the **untruncated** thermal state of mean x. Pure loss maps tau_(a barN) to tau_(b barN).

For a>b>0, the thermal logarithm and energy identities give exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
S(B^n)-S(E^n)
&=n[g(a\bar N)-g(b\bar N)]\\
&\quad-\left[
D(\rho_{B^n}\Vert\tau_{a\bar N}^{\otimes n})
-D(\rho_{E^n}\Vert\tau_{b\bar N}^{\otimes n})
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

The bracket is nonnegative by relative-entropy contraction under the product loss channel. Combining with (3) gives an upper bound for every allowed helper measurement and input. The function g(aN)-g(bN) is increasing when a>b, so the actual-energy bound is no larger than the budget-N expression.

This is the established thermal-reference/relative-entropy technique used in energy-constrained channel capacities [WQ18, Theorem 6]. We are not claiming a new Gaussian-extremality principle. Its application here becomes an assisted-channel converse because (1) remains valid after the helper measurement. E is not the full complement of B alone, so the usual point-to-point pure-loss capacity cannot simply be quoted with E substituted for that complement.

For b=0, E is vacuum and the bound is the ordinary maximum entropy S(B^n)<=n g(aN). For N=0, the signal is vacuum and the rate is zero. Finite output energy makes the relevant entropies finite. The standard average-energy-constrained coherent-information converse and coding equivalence [WQ18, Theorems 2 and 5] supply the channel-coding step; no special isometric-encoder premise is silently imposed on competing codes.

### 4.3 Matching achievability respects the photon budget

For a thermal average input tau_N,

\[
S(B)=g(aN),\quad S(E)=g(bN),\quad S(BD)=g((1-b)N).
\]

The BD modes can be combined passively into a mode with transmissivity a+c=1-b and a vacuum mode. Since 1-b>=a and g is increasing, the finite-dimensional minimum-cut expression would give exactly (5).

To justify the infinite-dimensional limit, truncate the *input used for achievability*, not the physical channel or the competing encoders in the converse. Put r=N/(1+N), epsilon_K=r^(K+1), and

\[
p_n^{(K)}=\frac{(1-r)r^n}{1-\epsilon_K}\ \ (0\le n\le K).
\]

Its actual mean is

\[
N_K=N-\frac{(K+1)\epsilon_K}{1-\epsilon_K}<N.
\tag{7}
\]

Vacuum passive loss never increases total photon number. For fixed K the involved input and output sectors are finite, so Section 2 supplies the required block measurement and achievable coherent information. Ordinary constrained coding can use the strict energy slack N-N_K. First take the coding limit for that fixed helper measurement and cutoff; then increase K. This avoids applying a finite-dimensional assistance theorem to an infinite state without a limit argument.

The entropies converge with a quantitative bound. For a loss fraction s, denote the truncated-input output entropy by H_s^(K). The discarded thermal tail has conditional mean K+1+N. Entropy concavity and the entropy-of-mixture bound give

\[
\boxed{
\max\!\left\{0,
\frac{g(sN)-h_2(\epsilon_K)-\epsilon_Kg(s(K+1+N))}{1-\epsilon_K}
\right\}
\le H_s^{(K)}\le\frac{g(sN)}{1-\epsilon_K}.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Use these bounds at s=a,b,1-b. They enclose the finite-input assistance rate
min{H_a^(K),H_(1-b)^(K)}-H_b^(K) and converge to (5). Mere trace-distance convergence is not substituted for entropy control. No minimum-output-entropy conjecture or arbitrary Gaussian-measurement assumption is required.

For a=.2,b=.08,c=.72,N=1, K=12 has N_K=.9984128922 and finite-input min-cut rate .3682584013. Equation (8) already gives the conservative achievable lower bound .3665006250. At K=40 that lower bound is .368604593395, within about 2.2e-11 of the target formula. These are numerical evaluations of analytical bounds, not rational interval certificates, and not finite-code rates obtained by simulating the helper measurement.

## 5. Physical interpretation and numerical scale

For 80% loss, a=.2. Let eta denote the fraction of that lost field collected by the helper, so b=.8(1-eta) and c=.8 eta. Equation (5) gives:

| Collected fraction eta | Rate at N=1 photon/use | Rate at N=10 photons/use |
|---:|---:|---:|
| .50 | 0 | 0 |
| .75 | 0 | 0 |
| .80 | .1086251414 | .2556723299 |
| .90 | .3686045934 | .9709505945 |
| 1.00 | .7800269060 | 2.7548875022 |

All entries are qubits per signal mode. The unmonitored pure-loss channel at survival .2 has zero quantum capacity [WQ18]. The rates in the last row are consistent with known *full*-environment assistance [SVW05], not a new endpoint discovery. Optical rates exceeding one are allowed because the input is an oscillator rather than a qubit; their photon budgets are explicit.

When b>0 and a>b,

\[
\boxed{\sup_{N<\infty}Q_{\rm optical}^{\rm meas}(N)=\log_2(a/b).}
\tag{9}
\]

In particular, at a=.2,b=.08 the rate saturates toward log2(2.5)=1.3219280949 as energy increases. More photons cannot compensate without limit for unobserved loss, and no photon budget moves the a=b boundary. Equation (9) is an infinite-energy supremum, not a rate attained at finite power.

The energy law is increasing and concave, with

\[
\frac{d^2Q}{dN^2}=\frac{b-a}{\ln2\,N(1+aN)(1+bN)}<0.
\]

There is also the numerical capacity identity

\[
Q_{\rm optical}^{\rm meas}(N;a,b,c)
=Q_{\rm pure\ loss}\!\left(\frac{a}{a+b},(a+b)N\right)
\quad(a>b).
\]

This is an identity of optimal values after a resource rescaling, **not** a physical conversion of one channel into the other with free energy or an explicit decoder. The point-to-point pure-loss formula on the right is established [WQ18].

Coherent delivery of D is a different resource. It combines BD into pure loss of survival 1-b and yields
max{g((1-b)N)-g(bN),0}. At a=.2,b=.08,N=1 this is 1.5061724995 qubits/use, versus .3686045934 with measurement-only aid. This gap is not a claim that every practical measurement loses that exact amount relative to a real quantum optical receiver; both quantities are optimized asymptotic capacities in their respective tasks.

The helper itself is a quantum device. It may store and jointly process collected optical modes and use non-Gaussian operations. Only its message to B is classical. No finite-memory, measurement-depth, loss-at-helper, message-rate, or apparatus-energy bound is included. The theorem does not describe continuous feedback restoring the emitter during decay or a two-way secret-key capacity. It cannot be compared with an unassisted repeaterless bound while omitting the helper resource.
